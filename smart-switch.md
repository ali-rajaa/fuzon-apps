### Back up first

Before any transfer, make sure the things you can't replace are kept somewhere else too: photos and videos, important documents, and anything you'd be upset to lose. A transfer moves data between two phones, and it's always wise to have a second copy. Our [Cloud Storage app]({{ '/cloud-storage' | relative_url }}) is one way to keep a copy in the cloud.

### Charge both phones

Moving a lot of data takes time, especially years of photos and videos. Start with both phones well charged, or plug them in while they work, so neither runs flat halfway through.

### Make room on the new phone

Check the new phone has enough free space for everything you're moving. If space is tight, use Transfer File to move the most important things first, and leave old downloads and files you no longer need behind.

### Keep the phones together and leave them alone

Keep both phones close to each other until the transfer is done. Don't lock, restart or switch either phone off part way through, and avoid using them for other things while they work. Interrupting a transfer can leave it incomplete.

### Don't rush a big transfer

If you have years of photos and videos, start the transfer when you won't need either phone for a while, such as in the evening. Rushing to finish before you head out is how transfers get interrupted.

### Sign back into your apps

Some apps ask you to sign in again on a new phone, even after your data has moved across. Have your passwords ready, or a password manager, before you start. Banking and other security-sensitive apps often need to be set up fresh on each new phone.

### Know what can need extra steps

Some things live inside individual apps or accounts rather than on the phone itself, so they can need extra steps on a new phone. Chat apps often have their own backup and restore, and authenticator apps usually need to be set up again. Check how your most important apps move to a new phone before you reset the old one.

### Check everything arrived

Before you do anything to the old phone, open the new one and look through your photos, files and apps. Make sure the things you care about are there. It's much easier to fix a missing item while you still have the old phone in hand.

### Live with the new phone for a day

Once the transfer is done, use the new phone for a day before you hand the old one on. Make a call, send a message, open your banking app and take a photo. Everyday use is the best way to spot anything you've missed.

### Before your old phone moves on

Once you're happy with the new phone, sign out of your accounts on the old one, then reset it to factory settings before you sell it, trade it in or hand it down. Take out the SIM card and any memory card, and the old phone is ready for its next owner.

### Recycle the old phone if it's not going to a new owner

If your old phone isn't being sold or handed down, don't put it in the bin. Phones contain materials that can be recovered, and in Australia the MobileMuster program offers free mobile phone recycling, with drop-off points at many retailers and councils. Reset the phone first, then drop it off.
