### Decide what to keep

Start with the files that would be hardest to replace: family photos and videos, scanned documents, receipts and warranties, and the school and work files you'd need again. Those are worth keeping somewhere other than one phone. Things you can download again whenever you like, such as apps or music from a streaming service, usually don't need a second home.

### Give your folders simple names

A folder structure only helps if you can remember it. Keep top-level names short and obvious, like *Photos 2026*, *House*, *Car*, *Work* and *School*, and add a folder for a trip, an event or a project inside them when it needs one. Putting the date first, as in *2026-10 Margaret River*, keeps folders in order.

### Name files so you can find them later

Your camera gives photos names like IMG_4821, which tell you nothing six months later. When you upload something important, give it a clear name that says what it is and when it's from, like *2026-03 Car insurance renewal* or *Year 5 excursion form*. Clear names and simple folders together mean you can find a file in seconds instead of scrolling through hundreds.

### Make uploading a habit

Cloud storage works best when it's kept up to date. Upload new photos straight after an event, while you still remember which ones matter, or set a monthly reminder to catch up. A few minutes regularly saves a long clean-up later.

### Keep personal and work files apart

If you keep both personal and work files, give each its own top-level folder. It keeps family photos away from documents you share with colleagues, and makes it much harder to send the wrong file to the wrong person.

### Tidy up once in a while

Every few months, look through your cloud storage and clear out what you no longer need: duplicate photos, blurry shots, old drafts and expired documents. A tidy space is quicker to browse, and the files that matter stay easy to see.

### Share with care

Before you share, check you've picked the right file and the right person. Think about what's inside it: photos can show where you live or where your children go to school, and documents can include account numbers or addresses. Share only what the other person needs.

### Use Wi-Fi for big uploads

Photos and videos can be large. Uploading a big batch over Wi-Fi saves your mobile data, which matters after a holiday with hundreds of photos. If you're on mobile data, check your plan's allowance first.

### Keep your phone locked and your passwords private

Lock your phone with a PIN, pattern or fingerprint, and use a password you don't use anywhere else. Never share your password with anyone, including people who contact you claiming to be support. If you sell or give away a phone, sign out of your accounts and reset it first.

### Check before you delete anything from your phone

If you plan to remove files from your phone after uploading them, open your cloud storage first and check they're there and open properly. Only then delete the copies on the phone.

### Keep more than one copy of what really matters

For files you truly can't lose, keep more than one copy, for example in cloud storage and on a computer or an external drive. No single place should be the only home for something irreplaceable.
